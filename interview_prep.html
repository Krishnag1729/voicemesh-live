The 30-second pitch

You speak English into a cheap Android phone and hear Hindi speech back, typically within about 1.1 s (p95) of when you stop talking. The phone stays thin: it captures audio, uses Silero VAD to send only speech, compresses it with Opus and streams it over a WebSocket. A gateway routes each session to a GPU worker that runs a streaming FastConformer ASR, a clause segmenter, IndicTrans2 translation and Piper Hindi TTS. One RTX 3050 lab PC serves 210 concurrent users at about $0.002 per user-hour. The app is 7.1 MB and uses ~64 MB of memory while streaming.

The one-line takeaway: translate clause by clause instead of waiting for whole sentences, and keep every heavy model on one shared GPU so a 2 GB phone only has to listen and play.

Architecture, step by step

  1. Phone (Kotlin, foreground service): AudioRecord 16 kHz → Silero VAD gate with hangover and pre-roll → MediaCodec Opus ~16 kbit/s → OkHttp WebSocket. Hindi Opus comes back into a jitter buffer and AudioTrack.
  2. Gateway (Python asyncio): auth token, admission control at ≤85% predicted load, sticky routing to one worker, a 6 s replay buffer and a resume token for dropped connections.
  3. Workers dial out to the gateway with an HMAC token, so a lab machine never has to open a port.
  4. Streaming ASR: FastConformer-114M, 160 ms chunks, cache-aware, greedy CTC, batched across all users on a slot arena (2.6 MB of state per stream). Silent chunks skip the encoder.
  5. Clause segmenter commits a clause on punctuation, a 0.4 s pause, an opener word or 12 words. The last word of a partial is never committed.
  6. IndicTrans2 dist-200M translates each clause (int8 on CPU, or fp16 ONNX on the GPU with continuous batching).
  7. Piper Hindi TTS speaks it (CPU process pool, or a CPU front plus a TensorRT fp16 back on the GPU). Audio is Opus-encoded and streamed back.
  8. An EDF gate holds MT and TTS GPU work whenever an ASR tick has the earlier deadline, so ASR stays real time on a shared card.

Key design decisions and why

DecisionWhy
Thin phone, models on a serverA 2 GB Go phone can't hold ASR + MT + TTS. Measured on-phone baseline was 2.1 s first audio. The phone only does VAD and Opus.
Translate by clause, not sentenceLong sentences would make you wait. Clauses start Hindi while you are still talking. Agreement with sentence-level translation is chrF++ 68.7 (E2).
VAD gate on the phoneOnly ~45% of chunks are sent: 54 KB/min uplink, and the GPU skips silence. Cost: +0.48 pp WER, within the accepted +0.5 pp (G2).
Workers dial out to the gatewayUniversity labs open no inbound ports. Dial-out also makes adding a node (the Windows laptop) a single command.
6 s replay buffer + resume tokenIf a worker dies, its sessions move to another node and replay the last 6 s, so no speech is lost (4/4 sessions resumed).
Move MT and TTS onto the GPU (M17–M18)On CPU, TTS limited capacity to 80 users while the GPU was ≥71% idle. GPU MT cut MT p95 from 1.1 s to 0.09 s.
TensorRT for Piper's back half, CPU for its frontThe front is ~2,400 tiny ops (faster on CPU). The vocoder back is 12× faster in TensorRT fp16. TTS went from ~50 to ~770 audio-s/s.
EDF gate, not CUDA stream priorities or MPS (M19)Measured: priorities and MPS gave no gain, because running blocks can't be preempted. EDF beat FCFS by 6–8 ms tick p95.
fp16 + CUDA graphs on the ASR encoder (M21)ASR became the limit at 160 users. Graphs plus fp16 cut a batch-64 step from 71.8 to 48.9 ms for +0.04 pp WER, raising capacity to 210.
Slot arena, not paged attentionFastConformer state is only 2.6 MB per stream, so copying is cheap. Paging was only ever measured on random weights, so it isn't claimed.
Privacy by designSpeech is never stored. Logs and metrics carry no text and no audio. Consent screen, adults only.

Likely questions, with short answers

Latency

How do you define “first audio”, and why is it under a second?

From when the speaker stops to when Hindi starts playing, per turn. It is often under 0.5 s because earlier clauses were already translated and spoken while the person was still talking. The p95 includes a fixed 0.29 s for the phone and network.

What is the slowest stage?

Not a model. It is the clause commit wait (p95 1.11 s): the segmenter waits for the next word, a 0.4 s pause or the VAD's end-of-speech before it commits a clause. All the models together take under 0.2 s.

Hindi is longer than English. Doesn't playback fall behind?

Yes, in long monologues: Hindi audio is ~1.16× the English duration, so the last clause of a long turn can start ~7 s after the speaker stops. A faster TTS rate (1.15) reduced it. In real conversation people take turns, which is what G1 measures.

GPU and scaling

How did capacity go from 80 to 210 users on one GPU?

Step by step, measuring each time: (1) MT to the GPU with continuous batching, (2) Piper's vocoder to TensorRT, (3) an EDF gate so MT/TTS never delay an ASR tick, (4) fp16 + bucketed CUDA graphs for the ASR encoder. A hidden memory ceiling was fixed too: PyTorch's allocator had reserved 5.4 GB for a 1.2 GB peak, solved with expandable_segments.

What limits it at 220 users?

Not the GPU anymore. ASR still meets its deadline, but the main Python process uses ~2 cores and MT p95 grows. The next step would be splitting the process or more GPUs.

How does admission control work?

Each node reports per-stage load every 2 s. The gateway predicts load as the maximum of sessions/capacity and the busiest stage, and refuses a new user above 85%, so existing users keep their latency instead of everyone getting slow.

Why EDF and not just GPU stream priorities?

I measured priorities, MPS and separate processes: none helped, because the GPU can't preempt a running kernel. EDF decides before launch, holding MT/TTS work whose deadline is later than the running ASR tick.

The phone

How did the app get to 7 MB and 64 MB of memory?

R8 shrinking, arm64 only, no Compose/AppCompat, and a custom ONNX Runtime build containing only the VAD's operators. A gotcha: ORT's optimiser adds ops (FusedConv, Split) at load time, so the op list must come from the optimised graph. Backgrounding the app frees ~47 MB of window surfaces.

Has it run on a real 2 GB Go phone?

Not yet. It ran on a real Samsung (Android 15) and on a 2 GB emulator (PSS 83 MB). A true Go device is an open item.

Why does the website work in a browser too?

For the demo: the same server path is reachable from a browser page on GitHub Pages, which streams mic audio through a bridge that runs the same Silero gate. Audio goes over an ngrok tunnel to the lab PC.

Mesh and reliability

What happens if a worker crashes mid-sentence?

The gateway sees the dropped link (0.05–0.10 s on a reset; pings catch a silent death in ≤10 s), moves every session on that node to another node and replays the last 6 s of uplink audio. Tested with SIGKILL: 4/4 sessions resumed. Known gap: the user may hear up to 6 s of Hindi twice.

How do you add capacity?

Start another worker anywhere with outbound internet: it dials the gateway and starts taking sessions. A Windows laptop node was added this way with one PowerShell script.

Quality

Doesn't the VAD gate hurt recognition?

Slightly: +0.48 pp WER (4.05 → 4.53%) over 2,739 words, because skipping silence shifts the encoder's cache. We accepted ≤+0.5 pp in exchange for sending ~45% of the audio. The margin is thin, and I say so.

Does clause-by-clause translation lose quality?

Some. Joined clause translations agree with sentence translations at chrF++ 68.7. On FLORES-200 clause mode scores 52.5 vs 58.1 for sentences. There is no human Hindi reference for our speech, so that is agreement, not adequacy.

Did fp16 or the GPU change the translation?

No measurable loss: GPU fp16 chrF++ is within +0.15 of CPU int8, with identical text on the probe sentences.

How it was built

How did you use the AI coding assistant?

Claude Code wrote and ran most of the code. I set the goals and made the key calls (the definition of first audio, the G2 criterion, the capacity rule, slot arena vs paging, TensorRT vs CUDA EP). A fixed SPEC.md, a PLAN.md with one acceptance test per milestone, an append-only INSIGHTS.md log and a short STATUS.md handoff kept it on track across sessions. The full development transcript is published with the project.

What did you learn?

Measure, don't assume: the GPU was 71% idle while I thought it was busy, stream priorities didn't help, and a failed hypothesis is a result. And the report is generated from the CSVs, so no number can be typed in by hand.

Known limits and what's next

  • Not yet tested on a real 2 GB Android Go phone, and radio energy use is unmeasured.
  • Piper Hindi voices are research-only (CC-BY-NC-SA or unverified); the commercial fallback is the Android system Hindi voice.
  • Capacity 210 is not soak-tested yet; the main process memory grows ~4.6 MB/min during a soak.
  • Cloud cost is extrapolated from the lab GPU. Next (M22): measure on L4/A10/A100.
  • Release signing uses a debug key; a real key is needed for a store release.

Demo plan (about 10 min)

  1. Open the live page, play a sample clip: Hindi starts within a second of the clip ending.
  2. Speak into the mic: show the English and Hindi text appearing clause by clause.
  3. Show the Android app on the phone: START, speak, Hindi plays; STOP is clean.
  4. Kill a worker during a session: the session moves to the other node and continues.
  5. Show the generated report: gates G1–G4 and the 80 → 160 → 210 capacity table.
